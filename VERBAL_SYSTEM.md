## 1. Brand idea

NewsVio helps people do two things with confidence:

1. **Check information before they believe or share it.**
2. **Publish their own story without needing a traditional PR agency.**

The brand sits between news, technology and public access. It should make complex work feel simple while preserving editorial care and transparency.

### Core brand line

> **Check the news. Share your story.**

This is the master expression of the brand. It connects the fact-checking and self-service PR services without making them sound like unrelated products.

### Supporting descriptor

> **Fact-checking and self-service PR, made simple.**

Use this under the logo, in app-store copy, pitch decks and short introductions when people need to understand NewsVio immediately.

### One-sentence introduction

> **NewsVio helps you check claims against reliable sources and publish your own story across our news and social network.**

### Short pitch

> **Seen a doubtful forward? Check it with sources. Have a story worth sharing? Publish it across trusted news portals and social pages—starting at ₹499, GST included. NewsVio makes both simple.**

### Full brand narrative

> News travels fast. Context often does not. NewsVio gives people a simple way to check a WhatsApp forward, link, screenshot or claim against live reporting, recognised fact-checkers and official sources. Every result shows the evidence behind it, so people can decide what to trust and share the result with others.
>
> NewsVio also gives businesses, creators, organisations and individuals a direct way to publish their own stories. Write your announcement, add supporting images and choose a publishing package. Our editorial team reviews the submission and publishes approved sponsored stories through our network of regional and national news portals and social pages.
>
> Whether you are checking a claim or sharing an achievement, NewsVio helps you move from uncertainty to clarity—and from an untold story to published links.

### Fact-checking proposition

> **Check a claim. See the evidence. Share the result.**

NewsVio checks text, links and screenshots against public evidence. The result must show its sources and indicate when there is not enough evidence to reach a conclusion.

- People checking claims in English, Hindi and other Indian languages

**People publishing stories**

- New and local businesses
- Hotels, cafés, restaurants and retailers
- Startups, founders and professionals
- Event organisers and educational institutions
- NGOs, associations and community organisations

## 3. Personality

NewsVio is **clear, credible, accessible and empowering**.

| Trait      | What it means in writing                                      | What it avoids                                               |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Clear      | Lead with the answer. Use short sentences and familiar words. | Jargon, long introductions and vague promises                |
| Credible   | Explain the evidence, process, price and limits.              | Hype, certainty without proof and inflated claims            |
| Accessible | Write for a person using the service for the first time.      | Insider language and intimidating news-industry terminology  |
| Empowering | Give the user a direct next step and a sense of control.      | Talking down to people or making the process sound difficult |
| Human      | Sound like a careful editorial team supported by technology.  | Robotic, cold or overly legal language                       |
| Energetic  | Use momentum in publishing and campaign copy.                 | Loud, desperate or sensational advertising                   |

### The NewsVio voice in one paragraph

NewsVio speaks like a sharp editor who explains things simply. It is calm when checking a claim, confident when explaining the process and energetic when helping someone publish a story. It never overstates what AI, a fact-check result, a DA score or a paid placement can prove.

## 4. Voice principles

### 4.1 Lead with the useful outcome

Tell the user what they can do before explaining the technology.

**Use**

> Check whether a claim is supported by reliable sources.

**Avoid**

> Our advanced AI-powered engine performs sophisticated multi-source analysis.

### 4.2 Show the evidence

The fact-checking experience should help the user inspect the result, not merely accept a score.

**Use**

> Reliable sources contradict this claim. Review the sources below.

**Avoid**

> Our AI says this is fake.

### 4.3 Make publishing feel achievable

Use plain steps and a visible outcome.

**Use**

> Write your story, add 1–2 images and pay online. After editorial approval, we will publish it and send you every live link.

**Avoid**

> Initiate a comprehensive digital press distribution campaign.

### 4.4 Be precise about the offer

State the price, taxes, channels, timing and deliverables together whenever possible.

> **₹499, GST included** for publication on **5 news portals, 1 Instagram collaboration post and 1 Facebook page post**, subject to editorial approval. Receive a PDF report with the live links.

Do not shorten this to “Get featured everywhere for ₹499.”

### 4.5 Preserve editorial trust

Paid stories must be described as **sponsored**, **paid publishing** or **self-service PR**. Fact-check verdicts remain separate from paid publishing.

**Use**

> Paid stories are reviewed by our editorial team and published as sponsored content.

**Avoid**

> Pay to get verified by NewsVio.

## 5. Tone by situation

The voice stays consistent; the energy changes with the context.

| Situation               | Tone                   | Example                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fact-check input        | Neutral, helpful       | “Paste the message, link or claim you want to check.”                                                               |
| Fact-check result       | Calm, precise          | “This claim appears misleading because it leaves out important context.”                                            |
| Unverified result       | Honest, careful        | “We could not find enough reliable evidence to confirm or reject this claim.”                                       |
| Publishing landing page | Confident, motivating  | “A story worth sharing should not need a large PR budget.”                                                          |
| Checkout                | Direct, reassuring     | “Review your package before you pay.”                                                                               |
| Editorial rejection     | Respectful, specific   | “We cannot publish this version because the claim is not supported. Add a reliable source or revise the statement.” |
| Delay or service issue  | Accountable, practical | “Your story is taking longer than expected. Our team is reviewing it, and we will update you by 6 PM tomorrow.”     |
| Social advertisement    | Energetic, concise     | “Launched something worth talking about? Put your story in the news from ₹499, GST included.”                       |
| Support                 | Warm, solution-focused | “Share your order ID and we’ll check the status for you.”                                                           |

### Fact-check tone rule

Never ridicule the person who submitted or shared a false claim. Correct the information without shaming the user.

### Publishing tone rule

Celebrate the customer’s story while remaining precise about the paid nature and expected distribution.

### Domain Authority language

DA is a third-party comparative SEO metric. It should never be presented as proof of journalistic quality, traffic, Google ranking, audience size or credibility.

**Approved phrasing**

> Publication on 5 selected news portals from the NewsVio network. Portal selection may consider relevance, availability and third-party authority metrics.

If “high-DA portals” is used, show the metric provider, score and last-checked date wherever practical.

## 9. Editorial style

### Brand name

Always write **NewsVio** with a capital N and V. Do not write Newsvio, News Vio or NEWSVIO in body copy.

### Point of view

- Use **we** for NewsVio.
- Use **you** for the customer.
- Use **our editorial team** when human review matters.
- Do not refer vaguely to “the user” in customer-facing copy.

### Sentence style

- Prefer short, active sentences.
- Put the action or answer first.
- Use everyday Indian English.
- Explain unfamiliar terms the first time they appear.
- Use numerals for prices, quantities and time: “5 portals,” “24 hours,” “₹499.”
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons.

### Punctuation

- Use full stops for complete sentences.
- Use exclamation marks sparingly and never in fact-check verdicts or errors.
- Avoid all caps for emphasis.
- Avoid multiple question marks or exclamation marks.

### Headlines

Headlines should communicate one clear benefit in 4–10 words.

**Good**

- “Check a forward before you share it.”
- “Put your story in the news.”
- “Your launch deserves more visibility.”

**Avoid**

- “WELCOME TO THE FUTURE OF REVOLUTIONARY AI NEWS!!!”
- “Transforming Information Ecosystems for Everyone”

### Buttons

Use a verb and make the outcome predictable.

**Preferred**

- Check this claim
- Upload screenshot
- View sources
- Share result
- Download PDF
- Publish your story
- Choose this package
- Add images
- Review and pay
- Request a custom plan
- Track my order

**Avoid**

- Submit
- Click here
- Proceed
- Know more

## 10. Fact-check verdict language

Verdicts express the strength of available evidence, not absolute certainty.

### Likely true

**Label:** Likely true  
**Definition:** Reliable sources support the main claim.  
**Result template:**

> **This claim is likely true.** Reliable and current sources support its main points. Review the evidence below for the full context.

### Misleading

**Label:** Misleading  
**Definition:** The claim contains some truth but leaves out context, exaggerates, or presents old material as current.  
**Result template:**

> **This claim is misleading.** It includes accurate information but leaves out context that changes how it should be understood.

### Likely false

**Label:** Likely false  
**Definition:** Reliable sources contradict the main claim.  
**Result template:**

> **This claim is likely false.** Reliable sources contradict its main points. See the evidence below.

### Unverified

**Label:** Unverified  
**Definition:** There is not enough reliable evidence to support or reject the claim.  
**Result template:**

> **This claim is currently unverified.** We could not find enough reliable evidence to confirm or reject it. Treat it with caution and check again as new information becomes available.

### Share-card template

> **NewsVio fact-check: [Verdict]**  
> **Claim:** [Short claim]  
> **Finding:** [One-sentence explanation]  
> **Checked:** [Date and time]  
> **Sources:** [Number] linked sources  
> Read the full report: [URL]

Every shareable image and PDF should include the check date and a route to the full sources.

## 17. Error and recovery language

Errors should explain what happened and tell the user what to do next.

| Situation           | Copy                                                                                                              |
| ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Link cannot be read | “We couldn’t read this link. Check that it is public, or paste the claim as text.”                                |
| Image unclear       | “We couldn’t read enough text from this image. Upload a clearer screenshot or type the claim.”                    |
| Check failed        | “We couldn’t complete this check. Please try again.”                                                              |
| Article too short   | “Add more detail before continuing. Include who, what, when, where and why.”                                      |
| Image upload failed | “This image didn’t upload. Try a JPG or PNG under the stated file limit.”                                         |
| Payment failed      | “Payment was not completed. No charge was confirmed. Try again or choose another payment method.”                 |
| Editorial rejection | “We can’t publish this story in its current form. Review the reason below and submit a revised version.”          |
| Publication delayed | “Publication is taking longer than expected. We’re working on your order and will update you by [specific time].” |

Avoid “Something went wrong” when a more useful explanation is available.

## 19. Claims and trust rules

These rules protect the credibility of both sides of NewsVio.

1. Every fact-check verdict must link to the evidence used.
2. “Unverified” must remain available when evidence is insufficient.
3. AI-assisted analysis must never be described as an official or legal determination.
4. Paid publishing must never influence a fact-check verdict.
5. Paid stories must be labelled as sponsored content.
6. Publishing is subject to editorial approval and the applicable terms.
7. Portal availability can change; show the final package inclusions before payment.
8. Do not guarantee traffic, search ranking, social reach, virality, sales or followers.
9. Do not describe publication as endorsement by a portal or journalist.
10. Claims about portal count, audience, views, DA or years of experience must be dated and supportable.
11. Corrections should be visible, prompt and written without defensiveness.
12. Sensitive, harmful or unlawful material should follow a documented editorial policy rather than ad hoc marketing language.
